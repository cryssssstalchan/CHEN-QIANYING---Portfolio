# Content Marketing Portfolio — Case Study Intake Guide

This portfolio is intentionally being built with placeholders. Replace a placeholder only when the relevant work and details are ready. Do not publish confidential client information or unverified performance claims.

## Suggested case study structure

1. **Project snapshot** — project name, date, brand or context, role, collaborators, and status.
2. **Brief & objective** — the challenge, business or communication goal, audience, and constraints.
3. **Audience insight** — what the audience needs, believes, searches for, or responds to; note the source of the insight.
4. **Strategy** — positioning, key message, content pillars, channel choice, and rationale.
5. **Creative execution** — final copy, images, design files, video, editorial plan, publishing examples, or links.
6. **Distribution** — channels, publishing cadence, formats, and any paid/owned/earned support.
7. **Results** — verified metrics, measurement period, baseline or target where available, and the source of the data.
8. **Reflection** — what worked, what did not, lessons learned, and the next iteration.

## Materials checklist

For each project, collect whatever is available; incomplete submissions are fine.

- Original brief, project background, objective, and target audience
- Copy, article drafts, social posts, scripts, content calendars, or campaign concepts
- Images, design files, screenshots, video links, and published URLs
- Your role and exact contribution; team contributions where relevant
- Channel, publication date, campaign duration, and distribution context
- Analytics screenshots or exports with metric names and date ranges
- Permission to display the work and any required attribution or anonymisation

## Reporting results responsibly

- Use only metrics supported by analytics, reports, or another identifiable source.
- Include the reporting window and platform when possible.
- Distinguish your own contribution from team or campaign-wide outcomes.
- If results are unavailable, describe deliverables and learning rather than inventing numbers.
- Label speculative concepts, student projects, and self-initiated work accurately.

## Suggested project categories

- Content strategy
- Social media and editorial content
- Brand storytelling and copywriting
- Campaign concepts and integrated campaigns
- Cross-cultural communication
- Content research and performance learning
